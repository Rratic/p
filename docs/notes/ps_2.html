    THE NATURE OF PSYCHOLOGY

    evaluate the validity of psychological claims?

    1. know what psychological facts are already firmly established. If the new claim is not compatible with those facts, you should be cautious.

    2. have the knowledge to determinewhether the arguments that support the new claim meet the standards of scientific evidence.

    what? examples:

    • prosopagnosia (brain)

    • fundamental attribution error: When explaining the behavior of others, people tend to overestimate the causal effect of personality traits and underestimate those of situational factors

    history:

    • Greek philosophers

    • nature–nurture debate

      • nature view: Descartes -> modern information-processing perspectives

      • nurture view: John Locke -> associationist psychology

    • scientific psychology

      • Wilhelm Wundt: introspection: observing & recording

    • structuralism & functionalism

      • E. B. Titchener: structuralism: the analysis of mental structures, pure analytic

      • William James: functionalism: how the mind works to enable an organism to adapt to and function in its environment

    • behaviorism & Gestalt psychology & psychoanalysis

      • John B. Watson: behaviorism: nearly all behavior is a result of conditioning and the environment shapes behavior by reinforcing specific habits

        • stimulus–response (S–R) psychology (a set of terms)

      • Gestalt for ferm/configuration, perceptual experiences depend on the patterns formed by stimuli and on the organization of experience

        • The whole is different from the sum of its parts, because the whole depends on the relationships among the parts.

      • Sigmund Freud: psychoanalysis: unconscious

    • twentieth-century

      • information-processing models

      • modern linguistics

      • neuropsychology
